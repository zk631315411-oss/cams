[v7u_N001009|1009] Fiat-collateralized stablecoins: Backed 1:1 by fiat reserves, such as euro, and stored in custodial accounts. The issuer holds an equivalent amount of fiat currency in reserve for every stablecoin issued. Risks include ease of conversion back into traditional fiat and regulatory gaps, making them attractive targets for money laundering.
ZH: 法币抵押稳定币由法定储备1:1支持，易转换回法币且存在监管漏洞，易被洗钱利用

[v7u_N001010|1010] Crypto-collateralized stablecoins: Backed by other cryptocurrencies rather than fiat. Their volatility can lead to liquidity issues, and criminals might use decentralized exchanges to obscure the origin of funds through complex transactions.
ZH: 加密抵押稳定币由其他加密货币支持，波动性可能导致流动性问题，犯罪分子可利用去中心化交易所掩盖资金来源

[v7u_N001011|1011] Algorithmic stablecoins: Maintain their peg through algorithms and market adjustments to supply and demand instead of being backed by fiat or crypto collateral. Money launderers might use algorithmic stablecoins to move funds rapidly, leveraging value fluctuations to disguise illicit asset transfers and complicate forensic tracking.
ZH: 算法稳定币通过算法和市场调整维持锚定，犯罪分子可利用其快速转移资金并掩盖非法资产转移

[v7u_N001012|1012] Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC): Government-issued digital currency, typically not classified as a stablecoin due to its direct link with state-issued fiat.
ZH: 央行数字货币（CBDC）是政府发行的数字货币，通常不被归类为稳定币

[v7u_N001013|1013] While CBDCs are stable by design, as they are directly backed by central banks and maintain parity with national currencies, weak AML and KYC regulations can pose financial crime risks.
